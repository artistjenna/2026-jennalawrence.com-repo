Reflection #3: Writing Online Content

Transforming content into engaging self-study digital lessons prepares us for building compelling lean content. This week we reflect on building a custom learning solution that helps product designers build error states in partnership with engineering.

5 to 7 minute read

Our second assignment

This week's blog is focused on how I'm growing through our second writing assignment. It's titled Writing online content and is one of the assignment outcomes from the Writing for Learner Engagement course which is part of the Learning Experience Design program I am taking through the Ontario Institute of Studies in Education (OISE) department at the University of Toronto.

Contents

  • What we did
  • How I'm growing

The ask

What we did

Assignment 2, Writing Online Content, is about transforming content into engaging self-study digital lessons. I opted to build a custom solution leveraging a piece of educational content I had the opportunity to create in recent years.

Above: Writing Assignment 2 - Writing Online Content title page with an example of a 504 Gateway time-out error from Medium.com.

Project at a glance:

Approach: Video, presentation, and exercise templates
Project title: Designing Error State Interfaces
Audience: Product Designers

Problem statement: Design a better way for designers and engineers to better anticipate and collaborate on error states so that error states are well designed for end users in time for engineering hand off.
Goal: Confidently plan for, collaborate on, and design solutions for thoughtful error state experiences that help users easily identify, diagnose and recover from expected and non-standard error states in user interfaces.
WIIFM:
  • Improving error state experiences for end users
  • Avoiding engineering timeline delays
  • Better anticipating engineering needs related to error state designs
  • Centralizing error states for unified experiences

The learnings

How I'm growing

How my thinking is evolving:

  • Anchoring this work in a relatable persona helped me get to the core goal for my learner.
    • Learner Goal Example: Design error states before engineering begins implementing the designs so that she is not pulled back into a project while the engineering team is working on our late night pull request.
  • Building a learning experience introduction or overview helped me think long and hard about communicating the benefits of the learning experience.
    • Example: Design error states before engineering begins implementing the designs so that you're not pulled back into a project while the engineering team is working on our late night pull request.
    • What to expect:
      • Improve error state experiences for users
      • Anticipate engineering needs to avoid delayed engineering timelines
      • Build for unified experiences
  • Chunking the content helped me consider where my solution needed to be trimmed down, reformatted, and reapproached.
  • Presenting insightful and captivating statistics at the beginning of the lesson helped me think more about evoking emotions early from the learner.
  • It's clear to me that I need to get creative when creating learning content and that the content needs to take the form of many shapes so that it keeps the learner engaged and helps them flex their new skills.
  • I'm pleased to see how writing lean content and considering the delivery format of my content is improving my writing skills.

Above: Excerpt from the Writing Assignment 2 - Writing Online Content showing a UX Product Designer Persona and includes a quote, “The best thing about design is how it supports people.”

Surprises that came up:

I had forgotten how dense this subject matter is and realize that I have an even bigger challenge ahead of me in future iterations of this learning program as I want to make it more accessible to folks who may have some experience with standard error states but are unfamiliar with non-standard or unexpected error states

How I am being challenged:

  • Writing good content for learning experiences is the core challenge for me because I have partnered with amazing editorial people in my career and they have set the bar high for the level of writing talent I'd like to achieve.
  • I keep having moments of reflection and growth where I ask myself:
    • “What are the benefits of this approach for our learners.”
    • “What's in it for my audience?”
    • “How can I make this more engaging by mixing it up?”
    • “How can I attach this to an existing piece of knowledge for the learner?”
  • Some of the major improvements made in this version of the learning experience included:
    • adding supporting metrics to evoke emotion early
    • infusing activities along the way to support the learner in their collaborations with their peers
  • Something I'd like to see myself better apply in future is using scaffolding to attach this piece of learning to the learner's existing knowledge because this subject could be pretty dense for those who are unfamiliar with the concept of http responses even though http responses are the foundation of hyper text transfer protocol (aka the internet.)

A story from my practice:

I began designing learning material for other designers on error states 5 years ago or more. It is a greatly misunderstood area of design and it is also greatly under served. This course has challenged me to put metrics behind the learning content to help others see the value in why error states should matter to them instead of being an after thought. It's clear that bad error states are costly and designers can take the time to learn how to better collaborate on them so that they come to the table prepared with solutions that work for engineering and also help solve unusual use cases. Asking me to think about the learning content through this lens has made this content more impactful and my hope is to continue improving it. The timeline for this project was short and so I felt I had to skip a few of the steps I would normally have taken to build out the supporting content in a better way. Thank you for challenging us this week because it set us up for success. I can feel myself growing new writing skills.

Enjoyable moments of delight

A lot of the work I do in my career is completed under non-disclosure agreements which prevent me from sharing some of my best work with others. I found it extremely enjoyable working through this as a class exercise because it is giving me the opportunity to improve a piece of work I wasn't able to take with me in previous roles. Having the ability to share this work with others is the best part of this assignment.

Next steps

What's next?

Things I can do in future

  • One of the things I know I'd like to do more of in the future is building more activities into my learning presentations and recording supporting video tutorials. I believe that this will help me engage the view and solidify their knowledge as they play safely using their new skills.
  • Designers are being recruited from various disciplines that don't include traditional design education or backgrounds. Knowing this helps us meet our learners' expectations in creative ways so that we can bring them along with us as the design space evolves to be more inclusive of individuals without formal design education.

Above: Excerpt from Writing Assignment 2 - Writing Online Content where learners can use a job aide template to collaborate with their engineering partners to identify error states.

Burning questions:

Some of the open questions I still have include:

  • How can companies make APIs accessible to designers so that they can self discover error states when engineers have not yet onboarded to the project?
    • I have yet to get the solution on this because rightfully so engineering needs to be involved to build solutions integrated with APIs for us to begin discovering possible error states.
  • How can we get engineers more involved in building better error states in partnership with design? I ask this one because on teams where I have been empowered to collaborate with engineering on error states we have found some really unusual examples and built exciting solutions to solve for them. On other teams where engineering is less engaged - I have witnessed them cutting corners on error states and even refusing to collaborate on error state enhancements.
    • Solutions: Good design requires good partnerships and advocacy from leadership. Anything we can do to incentivise these kinds of learning and collaboration is going to help employees build better solutions.

Conclusion:

Error states are something we should all care about because of the costly financial impacts to our business when error states are difficult to recognize, understand and recover from. As I rethink my approach these deep learning moments I am growing to better accommodate learners by keeping the content digestible and diversifying its presentation to keep learners engaged.
